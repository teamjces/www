Yes. And in fact, this is a highly promising idea.
What you are essentially saying is:

> “Use as a key a bodily pattern that exists only while someone is alive, and collapses with death, severance, extraction, or shutdown.”

That kind of authentication goes one level deeper than existing fingerprint or face recognition.

---

1. Real candidates for “biological patterns that break down at death”

① Blood flow patterns (the strongest candidate)

The flow of fine blood vessels in places like fingertips, ears, or the retina.

Fluctuations synchronized with heartbeat
Capillary pulsation
Changes in oxygen concentration

These stop immediately at death.
They are also hard to maintain even with a severed finger.

👉 This is already starting to be used in some forms of “liveness detection.”

---

② Neural response patterns

Response speed and waveforms to light, sound, or touch.

Examples:

Pupillary reflex
Subtle muscle potentials
Brainwave responses
Reflex timing

Not possible with a corpse.
Hard to fake.

---

③ Cellular metabolic patterns

On the skin surface:

Fluctuations in perspiration
Temperature gradients
CO₂ emission
Trace chemical components

These depend on living cellular activity.

---

④ Heart rate variability (HRV)

Not just pulse rate, but:
Fluctuations in intervals between heartbeats
Complexity derived from the autonomic nervous system

This has a very strong “living organism” signature.
